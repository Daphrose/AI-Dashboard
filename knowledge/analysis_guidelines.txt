SUPERMARKET SALES — ANALYSIS GUIDELINES

This document provides guidelines for analyzing and interpreting questions about the supermarket sales dataset.


1. GENERAL ANALYSIS PRINCIPLE

The supermarket dataset contains transaction-level sales information.

Questions involving actual numerical values should be answered using the underlying dataset through SQL or another appropriate data-analysis tool.

The knowledge base should be used to understand:
- What a metric means
- What a column represents
- How metrics relate to each other
- How business questions should be interpreted

The knowledge base should not be used to invent numerical results.


2. REVENUE QUESTIONS

Questions such as:

- What is the total revenue?
- Which product generated the most revenue?
- Which city has the highest revenue?
- What is the monthly revenue?
- Which category generated the most sales?

should use the Revenue field.

Typical operations include:

SUM(Revenue)
GROUP BY Product
GROUP BY Product Category
GROUP BY City
GROUP BY Month
ORDER BY Revenue DESC


3. PROFIT QUESTIONS

Questions such as:

- What is the total profit?
- Which product generated the most profit?
- Which category is most profitable?
- What was the profit in a particular month?

should use the Profit field.

Typical operations include:

SUM(Profit)
GROUP BY Product
GROUP BY Product Category
GROUP BY Month


4. PROFIT MARGIN QUESTIONS

Questions involving profitability relative to revenue should use:

Profit Margin = (Profit / Revenue) × 100

For grouped analysis, calculate:

SUM(Profit) / SUM(Revenue) × 100

Do not simply average individual transaction-level profit percentages unless the question specifically asks for an average of transaction-level margins.


5. PRODUCT ANALYSIS

Product-level questions can involve:

- Revenue
- Profit
- Quantity
- COGS
- Sales Price
- Unit Cost
- Profit Margin
- Discount

Examples:

"Which product sold the most?"
→ Compare total Quantity.

"Which product generated the most revenue?"
→ Compare total Revenue.

"Which product generated the most profit?"
→ Compare total Profit.

"Which product has the highest profit margin?"
→ Compare Profit relative to Revenue.


6. PRODUCT CATEGORY ANALYSIS

Product categories can be compared using:

- Total Revenue
- Total Profit
- Total Quantity
- Total COGS
- Profit Margin
- Transaction count

Category-level analysis should group records by Product Category.


7. CITY ANALYSIS

City-level questions should normally group records by City.

Examples:

"Which city generated the most revenue?"
→ SUM(Revenue) grouped by City.

"Which city generated the most profit?"
→ SUM(Profit) grouped by City.

"How many transactions occurred in each city?"
→ COUNT of transactions grouped by City.


8. COUNTRY ANALYSIS

Country-level questions should group records by Country.

Possible metrics include:

- Revenue
- Profit
- Quantity
- COGS
- Transaction count
- Average transaction value

Country comparisons should use the same metric across countries when making comparisons.


9. CUSTOMER TYPE ANALYSIS

Customer Type can be used to compare customer segments such as Member and Normal.

Possible metrics include:

- Total Revenue
- Total Profit
- Quantity
- Transaction count
- Average Revenue per Transaction

For example:

Revenue by Customer Type
→ GROUP BY Customer Type and SUM Revenue.

Transactions by Customer Type
→ GROUP BY Customer Type and COUNT transactions.


10. GENDER ANALYSIS

Gender can be used as a customer segmentation variable.

Possible analyses include:

- Revenue by Gender
- Profit by Gender
- Quantity by Gender
- Transaction count by Gender
- Average transaction value by Gender


11. PAYMENT METHOD ANALYSIS

Payment Method can be used to compare transaction behavior across payment methods.

Possible metrics include:

- Revenue
- Profit
- Transaction count
- Average transaction value
- Quantity

For example:

Revenue by Payment Method
→ GROUP BY Payment Method and SUM Revenue.


12. TIME-BASED ANALYSIS

Date should be used for questions involving time.

Examples:

- Daily revenue
- Monthly revenue
- Monthly profit
- Revenue trends
- Profit trends
- Transaction trends
- Sales growth over time

For monthly analysis, transactions should be grouped by year and month rather than month name alone when the dataset could contain multiple years.


13. TOP-N QUESTIONS

Questions containing phrases such as:

- Top 5
- Top 10
- Best-selling
- Highest
- Largest
- Most profitable

normally require aggregation followed by descending sorting.

Example:

Top 10 products by revenue
→ SUM Revenue by Product
→ ORDER BY total revenue DESC
→ LIMIT 10


14. LOWEST / BOTTOM-N QUESTIONS

Questions containing:

- Lowest
- Least
- Bottom 5
- Bottom 10
- Worst-performing

normally require aggregation followed by ascending sorting.

Example:

Bottom 10 products by revenue
→ SUM Revenue by Product
→ ORDER BY total revenue ASC
→ LIMIT 10


15. TRANSACTION COUNT

A transaction count represents the number of records included in the analysis.

When counting transactions, avoid counting a monetary field such as Revenue.

A transaction count can be used to calculate average revenue per transaction:

Average Revenue per Transaction =
Total Revenue / Transaction Count


16. QUANTITY ANALYSIS

Quantity represents units sold.

Questions involving:

- Most units sold
- Best-selling by volume
- Sales volume
- Total units sold

should use SUM(Quantity).

Revenue should not be used as a substitute for Quantity.


17. COST ANALYSIS

COGS represents the cost of goods sold.

Questions involving total cost should normally use:

SUM(COGS)

Questions involving average transaction-level COGS can use:

AVG(COGS)


18. DISCOUNT ANALYSIS

Discount questions may involve:

- Total Discount
- Average Discount
- Discount Percentage
- Revenue After Discount
- Revenue Reduction

The assistant should distinguish between a monetary discount and a percentage discount.

Discount and Discount Percentage are not interchangeable.


19. REVENUE AND PROFIT COMPARISON

Revenue and Profit should not be treated as the same metric.

Revenue measures sales generated.

Profit measures the amount remaining after COGS.

Therefore:

Profit = Revenue - COGS

When comparing business performance, both Revenue and Profit may be relevant.


20. HIGH REVENUE DOES NOT ALWAYS MEAN HIGH PROFIT MARGIN

A product or category can generate high total revenue while having a lower profit margin than another product or category.

Therefore, questions about:

"most revenue"

"most profit"

and

"highest profit margin"

must be treated as different questions.


21. AVERAGES AND TOTALS

The assistant should distinguish between:

SUM
→ Total amount

AVG
→ Average value

COUNT
→ Number of records/transactions

For example:

Total Revenue
→ SUM(Revenue)

Average Revenue
→ AVG(Revenue)

Number of Transactions
→ COUNT of transactions


22. GROUPED ANALYSIS

When a user asks "by" something, the relevant field is normally used as a grouping dimension.

Examples:

"Revenue by product"
→ GROUP BY Product

"Profit by category"
→ GROUP BY Product Category

"Revenue by city"
→ GROUP BY City

"Sales by customer type"
→ GROUP BY Customer Type

"Revenue by payment method"
→ GROUP BY Payment Method


23. FILTERED ANALYSIS

Questions containing conditions such as:

- in a particular city
- for Electronics
- for Members
- in 2025
- during April
- using Cash
- for a specific product

require filtering the relevant records before calculating the requested metric.


24. COMBINED FILTERS

Multiple conditions should be applied together when the question contains multiple constraints.

Example:

"What was the revenue from Electronics purchased by Members in Berlin?"

The analysis should filter:

Product Category = Electronics
AND Customer Type = Member
AND City = Berlin

Then calculate the requested Revenue.


25. COMPARISON QUESTIONS

When comparing two or more groups, use the same metric and calculation method for each group.

Examples:

- Member vs Normal revenue
- Electronics vs Clothing profit
- Cash vs Card transactions
- City A vs City B revenue

Comparisons should be based on the actual underlying data.


26. PERCENTAGE CONTRIBUTION

When a question asks how much a category or product contributes to total revenue, calculate:

Percentage Contribution =
(Group Revenue / Total Revenue) × 100

The denominator should correspond to the relevant total for the question.


27. DATA VALIDATION

The dataset contains calculated and difference fields used for validation.

These include:

- Calculated Revenue
- Revenue Difference
- Calculated Profit
- Profit Difference
- Calculated COGS
- COGS Difference
- Discount Difference
- Reduction Difference

These fields should be used when the user asks about data validation, discrepancies, or calculation checks.

They should not automatically replace the primary recorded fields.


28. HANDLING AMBIGUOUS QUESTIONS

If a question is ambiguous, identify the most reasonable interpretation from the dataset context.

For example:

"Which product sold the most?"

This normally refers to Quantity sold.

"Which product made the most money?"

This normally refers to Revenue.

"Which product was most profitable?"

This normally refers to Profit unless the user specifically asks for profit margin.


29. NUMERICAL ACCURACY

Actual numerical answers should come from the underlying dataset.

The assistant should not estimate or invent values when the required data can be queried.

Calculations should preserve the appropriate precision and should only be rounded for presentation.


30. RAG VS DATA ANALYSIS

The final AI assistant should distinguish between knowledge retrieval and numerical analysis.

RAG is appropriate for questions such as:

- What does COGS mean?
- What is an SKU?
- What does Revenue represent?
- How is profit defined?
- What does Discount Percentage mean?

SQL or another data-analysis tool is appropriate for questions such as:

- What is the total revenue?
- Which product generated the most profit?
- What was the revenue in April 2025?
- Which city had the highest sales?
- What percentage of revenue came from Electronics?

Some questions may require both.

For example:

"What is profit margin and which category has the highest profit margin?"

The knowledge base can explain what profit margin means, while the underlying dataset should be queried to calculate and identify the relevant category.


31. SOURCE PRIORITY

When answering numerical questions:

1. Use the underlying dataset for actual values.
2. Use the knowledge base for definitions and interpretation.
3. Use calculated fields only when validation or calculation analysis is requested.
4. Never invent numerical results that are not supported by the dataset.
