SUPERMARKET SALES DATASET — COLUMN DEFINITIONS

This document defines the 28 columns in the cleaned supermarket sales dataset. These definitions are intended to help the RAG system understand the meaning and analytical use of each field.


1. Date
The date on which the transaction occurred. It is used for time-based analysis such as daily, monthly, and yearly revenue and profit trends.

2. City
The city where the transaction took place. It can be used to analyze revenue, profit, sales volume, and customer activity by city.

3. Country
The country associated with the transaction. It can be used for geographical comparisons of sales and profitability.

4. Customer Type
The type of customer making the purchase. The dataset contains customer categories such as Member and Normal. It can be used to compare purchasing behavior and revenue between customer types.

5. Gender
The gender associated with the customer. It can be used to analyze sales and purchasing patterns by gender.

6. Product Category
The broad category to which a product belongs. Examples include Electronics, Clothing, Groceries, Personal Care, Household Items, and Stationery. It is useful for category-level revenue, profit, quantity, and margin analysis.

7. Product
The specific product purchased in the transaction. It can be used to identify top-selling products and compare product-level revenue and profitability.

8. SKU
SKU stands for Stock Keeping Unit. It is an identifier used to distinguish individual products or product variants. It can be used to identify and track specific products.

9. Unit Cost
The cost associated with one unit of the product. It represents the cost incurred by the business for each unit before sale.

10. Sales Price
The selling price associated with the product. It represents the price at which the product is sold per unit.

11. Quantity
The number of units purchased in the transaction. It can be used to measure sales volume and calculate aggregate quantities sold.

12. COGS
COGS stands for Cost of Goods Sold. It represents the cost associated with the goods sold in the transaction. It is used in profitability calculations.

13. Discount
The discount value applied to the transaction or product sale. It can be analyzed to understand the relationship between discounts, revenue, and profitability.

14. Payment Method
The method used by the customer to pay for the transaction. It can be used to compare revenue, transaction counts, and purchasing behavior across payment methods.

15. Revenue
The recorded revenue generated by the transaction. It is the primary measure used to analyze sales performance.

16. Profit
The recorded profit generated by the transaction. For this dataset, profit is validated as Revenue minus COGS.

17. Calculated Revenue
A revenue value calculated independently from other transaction fields for validation and comparison with the recorded Revenue field. It is used to investigate whether the recorded revenue agrees with the expected calculation.

18. Revenue Difference
The difference between the recorded Revenue and the independently calculated revenue. It is used to identify and investigate discrepancies between recorded and calculated revenue.

19. Calculated Profit
A profit value calculated independently from the relevant transaction fields for validation and comparison with the recorded Profit field.

20. Profit Difference
The difference between the recorded Profit and the independently calculated Profit. It is used to validate the consistency of the recorded profit values.

21. Revenue After Discount
The revenue value after accounting for the applicable discount. It is used to analyze the effect of discounts on realized revenue.

22. Discount Difference
The difference between the expected or calculated discount effect and the recorded discount-related value. It is used for data validation and consistency checking.

23. Calculated COGS
A COGS value calculated independently from the relevant transaction fields. It is used to validate and compare the calculated cost of goods sold with the recorded COGS.

24. COGS Difference
The difference between the recorded COGS and the independently calculated COGS. It is used to identify discrepancies and validate the COGS data.

25. Gross Sales
The gross sales amount before applicable reductions such as discounts. It can be used to compare sales before and after discounts.

26. Discount Percentage
The discount expressed as a percentage. It indicates the relative size of the discount applied to a sale.

27. Revenue Reduction
The amount by which revenue is reduced as a result of discounts or other applicable reductions. It helps measure the financial effect of discounts.

28. Reduction Difference
The difference between the expected or calculated revenue reduction and the recorded Revenue Reduction value. It is used for data validation and consistency checking.


IMPORTANT ANALYTICAL NOTES

- Revenue is the recorded revenue field and should be used as the primary revenue measure unless a question specifically asks about calculated revenue or validation.
- Profit is the recorded profit field and is validated as Revenue minus COGS.
- Calculated Revenue, Revenue Difference, Calculated Profit, Profit Difference, Calculated COGS, COGS Difference, Discount Difference, and Reduction Difference are primarily validation/calculation fields.
- Quantity represents the number of units sold, not revenue.
- Unit Cost represents the cost per unit, while COGS represents the cost associated with goods sold in the transaction.
- Sales Price represents the selling price per unit.
- SKU means Stock Keeping Unit and identifies a specific product or product variant.
- Discount Percentage should be interpreted as a percentage rather than a monetary amount.
- Revenue and Profit are monetary measures.
- The dataset can be analyzed by time, geography, customer characteristics, product, product category, payment method, revenue, cost, quantity, discount, and profitability.
