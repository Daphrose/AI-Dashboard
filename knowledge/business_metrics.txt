SUPERMARKET SALES — BUSINESS METRICS AND CALCULATIONS

This document explains the main business metrics used when analyzing the supermarket sales dataset. It provides definitions, relationships between metrics, and guidance on interpreting them.


1. REVENUE

Revenue is the recorded amount of money generated from sales transactions.

Revenue is the primary metric for measuring sales performance.

Revenue can be analyzed by:
- Date
- Month
- City
- Country
- Product
- Product Category
- Customer Type
- Gender
- Payment Method

When answering questions about total sales or total revenue, use the recorded Revenue field unless the user specifically asks for calculated or validation revenue.


2. COGS

COGS stands for Cost of Goods Sold.

COGS represents the cost associated with the goods that were sold.

It is an important cost metric because it allows the business to determine how much of its revenue remains after accounting for the cost of the goods sold.


3. PROFIT

Profit represents the amount remaining after subtracting COGS from Revenue.

For this dataset:

Profit = Revenue - COGS

Profit can be analyzed by:
- Product
- Product Category
- City
- Country
- Customer Type
- Payment Method
- Date


4. GROSS PROFIT MARGIN

Profit margin measures profit relative to revenue.

The standard calculation is:

Profit Margin = (Profit / Revenue) × 100

A higher profit margin means that a larger proportion of revenue remains as profit after accounting for COGS.

Profit margin should be interpreted together with revenue volume. A product can have a high profit margin but relatively low total profit if its revenue is small.


5. QUANTITY

Quantity represents the number of units sold.

Total quantity can be calculated by summing the Quantity field.

Quantity is useful for understanding sales volume independently of the monetary value of sales.

For example, a product may have high quantity but lower revenue if its selling price is relatively low.


6. UNIT COST

Unit Cost represents the cost associated with one unit of a product.

It can be used to understand the cost structure of individual products.


7. SALES PRICE

Sales Price represents the selling price associated with a unit of a product.

It can be compared with Unit Cost to understand the price and cost relationship.


8. DISCOUNT

Discount represents the monetary discount associated with a transaction or sale.

Discounts can reduce the amount of revenue realized from a sale.

Discount-related analysis can include:
- Total discount
- Average discount
- Revenue after discount
- Revenue reduction
- Discount percentage


9. DISCOUNT PERCENTAGE

Discount Percentage represents the discount expressed as a percentage.

It allows discounts to be compared across transactions with different monetary values.

A percentage discount is different from the monetary Discount field.


10. REVENUE AFTER DISCOUNT

Revenue After Discount represents revenue after accounting for the applicable discount.

It can be used to study how discounts affect realized revenue.


11. REVENUE REDUCTION

Revenue Reduction represents the reduction in revenue associated with discounts or other applicable reductions.

It can be used to quantify the financial effect of reductions on sales.


12. GROSS SALES

Gross Sales represents sales before applicable reductions such as discounts.

Comparing Gross Sales with Revenue After Discount can help analyze the effect of discounts on realized sales.


13. AVERAGE TRANSACTION VALUE

Average Transaction Value represents the average revenue generated per transaction.

It can be calculated as:

Average Transaction Value = Total Revenue / Number of Transactions

It can be used to compare customer groups, cities, payment methods, or other segments.


14. TOTAL REVENUE

Total Revenue is the sum of the Revenue field across the relevant transactions.

Example:

Total Revenue = SUM(Revenue)


15. TOTAL PROFIT

Total Profit is the sum of the Profit field across the relevant transactions.

Example:

Total Profit = SUM(Profit)


16. TOTAL COGS

Total COGS is the sum of the COGS field across the relevant transactions.

Example:

Total COGS = SUM(COGS)


17. TOTAL QUANTITY

Total Quantity is the sum of all units sold.

Example:

Total Quantity = SUM(Quantity)


18. NUMBER OF TRANSACTIONS

The number of transactions represents the number of records included in the analysis.

Example:

Number of Transactions = COUNT(Transactions)

In this dataset, each row represents a sales transaction.


19. AVERAGE REVENUE

Average Revenue represents the average revenue generated per transaction.

It can be calculated as:

Average Revenue = Total Revenue / Number of Transactions


20. AVERAGE PROFIT

Average Profit represents the average profit generated per transaction.

It can be calculated as:

Average Profit = Total Profit / Number of Transactions


21. AVERAGE COGS

Average COGS represents the average COGS associated with a transaction.

It can be calculated as:

Average COGS = Total COGS / Number of Transactions


22. REVENUE VS PROFIT

Revenue and profit measure different aspects of business performance.

Revenue measures the money generated from sales.

Profit measures what remains after COGS is deducted from revenue.

Therefore:

Profit = Revenue - COGS

A business can have high revenue without having equally high profit if its costs are high.


23. PROFITABILITY VS SALES VOLUME

High revenue does not automatically mean high profitability.

When evaluating products or categories, consider both:
- Total Revenue
- Total Profit
- Profit Margin
- Quantity Sold

A product with high revenue may generate the most total profit because of its large sales volume, even if another product has a slightly higher profit margin.


24. TIME-BASED METRICS

Revenue, profit, COGS, quantity, and transactions can be aggregated by time.

Examples include:
- Daily revenue
- Monthly revenue
- Monthly profit
- Monthly transaction count
- Monthly quantity sold

Monthly analysis is useful for identifying trends and changes in business performance over time.


25. SEGMENT ANALYSIS

Business metrics can be grouped by different dimensions.

Examples:

Revenue by Product Category
Profit by Product
Revenue by City
Revenue by Customer Type
Revenue by Payment Method
Profit Margin by Product Category
Quantity by Product
Transactions by Customer Type

Grouping allows the business to compare different segments of its operations.


26. DATA VALIDATION METRICS

Several columns exist specifically to support data validation.

These include:
- Calculated Revenue
- Revenue Difference
- Calculated Profit
- Profit Difference
- Calculated COGS
- COGS Difference
- Discount Difference
- Reduction Difference

These fields should not automatically replace the recorded business fields.

When a user asks for the official recorded revenue, profit, or COGS, use the corresponding recorded field.

Validation fields should primarily be used when investigating calculations, discrepancies, or data quality.


IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION RULE

The RAG assistant should distinguish between:

1. Metric definitions
2. Metric calculations
3. Actual metric values

Definitions and explanations can be retrieved from this knowledge document.

Actual numerical values should be obtained from the underlying dataset through the appropriate analytical/data-querying system rather than invented from the knowledge document.
